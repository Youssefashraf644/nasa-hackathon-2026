# Opportunity
Name: Mars Exploration Rover – Opportunity
Launch Date: July 7, 2003
Mission Duration: Landed January 24, 2004 (PST) / January 25, 2004 (EST); operated until June 10, 2018 (last communication; mission declared complete February 13, 2019)
Current Location: Perseverance Valley, rim of Endeavour Crater, Meridiani Planum, Mars (inactive; stationary at final resting place)

Three Key Discoveries:

1. Sedimentary rocks formed in ancient water: Opportunity was the first rover to identify and characterize sedimentary rocks on another planet, showing they formed in ancient ephemeral playas — evidence of past liquid water.

2. "Blueberries" and acidic groundwater: The rover discovered small hematite spheres nicknamed "blueberries" that formed from rising, acidic groundwater, revealing a wet and chemically active past.

3. Gypsum veins and clay minerals at Endeavour Crater: Opportunity found white gypsum veins indicating water flowing through underground fractures, and clay minerals formed in neutral-pH water at Endeavour Crater — the friendliest ancient conditions for microbial life found by either twin.

Fun Fact: Opportunity drove 28.06 miles (45.16 kilometers) on Mars, setting the off-world roving distance record in 2014 — a record it held until NASA's Perseverance rover surpassed it on October 11, 2026, reaching 28.10 miles (45.22 kilometers).

My Story:
I was the other twin, and I landed on the opposite side of Mars at Meridiani Planum, where mineral deposits hinted at a wet history. 
I was supposed to last 90 days, but I kept going for more than 14 years — driving backward when a wheel misbehaved, shimmying out of sand traps, and surviving dust storms that dimmed my solar panels. 
I found "blueberries," gypsum veins, and clay minerals that formed in neutral water, painting a picture of a Mars that was once warmer, wetter, and potentially hospitable to life.
I drove farther than any robot on another world, and I sent back hundreds of thousands of images of Mars' stark beauty. In June 2018, a massive dust storm finally silenced me, but my record and my discoveries live on in every mission that followed.
