# Spirit
Name: Mars Exploration Rover – Spirit (Columbia Memorial Station)
Launch Date: June 10, 2003
Mission Duration: Landed January 4, 2004; operated until March 22, 2010 (last communication; mission declared complete May 25, 2011)
Current Location: Gusev Crater, Mars (inactive; stationary at final resting place, likely stuck in loose sand at "Troy")

Three Key Discoveries:

1. Evidence of past water at Gusev Crater: Spirit found rocks and soils indicating that water once flowed or was present at the landing site, including altered volcanic rocks and deposits suggesting past aqueous activity.

2. Hydrothermal and volcanic history: Spirit's studies of the Columbia Hills revealed rocks that had been chemically altered by water, including opaline silica and carbonate-rich materials, pointing to ancient hydrothermal environments potentially favorable for life.

3. Wind and dust processes on Mars: Spirit documented dust devils and wind-driven dust activity, helping scientists understand how winds shape the Martian surface and atmosphere.

Fun Fact: Spirit became stuck in loose sand at "Troy" in 2009 while driving backward (to protect a failing wheel), and despite extensive efforts to free it, the rover could not escape — it continued doing stationary science until it fell silent in 2010.

My Story:
I was one of two identical twins sent to opposite sides of Mars, and I landed at Gusev Crater, a place that looked like it might once have held a lake.
I climbed hills, studied strange altered rocks, and found signs that water — even hot, mineral-rich water — had once flowed here. 
I got stuck in a sand trap late in my life, but I kept working as a stationary science station for months afterward. 
I operated for more than six years, far beyond my planned 90 days, and I helped show that Mars was once warmer and wetter — and possibly habitable.
Today I sit silent in Gusev Crater, a twin pioneer whose lessons live on in every rover that followed.
