# Sojourner
Name: Mars Pathfinder (Lander: Carl Sagan Memorial Station; Rover: Sojourner)
Launch Date: December 4, 1996
Mission Duration: Operated from landing on July 4, 1997 until final data transmission on September 27, 1997
Current Location: Ares Vallis, Mars (no longer operational; stationary at landing site)

Key Discoveries:
1. Evidence of past liquid water: rounded pebbles and conglomerate rocks at the landing site indicate Mars once had flowing water under warm conditions.
2. Martian core size constraints: radio tracking data suggested Mars' metallic core radius exceeds 1,300 kilometers but is less than approximately 2,000 kilometers.
3. Magnetic dust and ancient water cycle: airborne dust is magnetic, with characteristics suggesting the magnetic mineral is maghemite; an active water cycle in the past may have leached iron from crustal materials.

Fun Fact:
During landing, Pathfinder, protected by airbags, bounced about 15 times like a giant beach ball, reaching heights of up to 15 meters, before coming to rest approximately 1 kilometer from the initial impact point.

My Story:
I was a small rover, no bigger than a microwave oven, and I arrived on Mars inside a lander that bounced across Ares Vallis like a beach ball before coming to rest. 
When I rolled off my ramp, I became the first wheeled vehicle to explore another planet. 
I was slow and careful, but I was curious. 
I studied rocks with my camera and my spectrometer, and I found pebbles that had been rounded by water long ago. 
That discovery helped scientists see that Mars had once been warm and wet. My mission was short — only a few months — but it changed how we think about the Red Planet. 
Today I sit silent in Ares Vallis, a small pioneer waiting for the day humans might come to visit.
